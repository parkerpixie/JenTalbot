My story

I came up through the work, not around it.

My career moved through administrative operations, commercial marketing operations, CRM reporting, sales operations, marketing automation, campaign execution, and systems improvement. That path matters because I understand the real-world mess underneath polished deliverables.

What changed

I no longer have to stop at "someone should build this."

For years, I learned systems because the work required it. Salesforce, Pardot, Account Engagement, HubSpot, Dynamics, reporting tools, HTML troubleshooting, data cleanup, documentation, and workflow design all entered my toolkit that way.

AI-assisted development changed the boundary. I can now prototype and ship lightweight applications, work in JavaScript, reason through data structures and state, test responsive interfaces, explore APIs and integrations, debug technical problems, and keep iterating from real user behavior.

  • I learn systems quickly because I look for the logic underneath the interface.
  • I care about the human before I care about the feature list.
  • I use AI to expand what I can execute, not to outsource judgment.
  • I am comfortable moving between strategy, operations, data, interface, and technical implementation.
  • I want the next version to be easier, clearer, and more useful than the last one.

The next chapter

AI expanded me from systems thinker to systems builder.

My career has always lived where marketing, systems, data, and human behavior overlap. AI widened that intersection enough that I can now take an idea from rough problem statement to functioning application.

GrizzlyJohn is the clearest proof. I shaped the product around a real person, built the mobile-first experience, created working interaction and data behavior, shipped it, watched it get used, and kept evolving it.

That is the capability my resume cannot fully unlock: I can understand the business problem and increasingly build the thing that solves it.
