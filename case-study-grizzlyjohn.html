GrizzlyJohn: a product people actually use.

A mobile-first personal product designed around one actual human, combining reflection, curiosity, recovery, exploration, memory, and gentle reasons to return.

See the product thinking →Back to case studies
✦

This is where my marketing brain and product brain meet: understand the person, identify useful friction to remove, build the experience, watch what they use, and improve from there.

Screenshot slot: add the strongest GrizzlyJohn mobile screen here once we choose the visual story for this case study.

The problem

Build something personal enough to be useful, but light enough to invite return.

GrizzlyJohn was not designed from a generic persona. It was designed around a real person and the things that matter to him: longtime sobriety, thoughtful reflection, podcasts, tiny adventures, park memories, curiosity, humor, and a gentle grizzly guide with a little National Park energy.

Todaydaily reflection, rotating Bear Wisdom, Side Quest, and an optional personal note
Wisdomcustom reflection cards with a thought, question, and tiny practice
Questrandom Side Quests, completion tracking, and trail-stamp milestones
Roampersonal park/place passport with visited, want-to-go, and memory fields
Campfire Radiopodcast discovery plus a local listening log for ideas worth keeping
Privacypersonal entries stay on the device in local storage in the current MVP

Product approach

Personalization starts with the human, not the feature list.

01

Understand

Start with what the person already values, returns to, talks about, and finds emotionally useful.

02

Shape the experience

Group those needs into a small set of useful behaviors rather than building a giant everything-app.

03

Build simply

Use a mobile-first, lightweight architecture with no framework or account requirement in the MVP.

04

Observe adoption

Pay attention to what gets used voluntarily. Repeat use is stronger evidence than a feature checklist.

05

Iterate

Let real behavior and feedback determine what deserves expansion, refinement, or removal.

What this proves

I can move from customer understanding to product adoption.

🧠

Behavioral design

The product is organized around motivations and habits rather than technology for technology's sake.

↻

Iteration

Real usage creates a feedback loop for deciding what to improve next.

♡

Adoption

The most important result so far is simple: the intended user returns to it and enjoys using it.

The takeaway

GrizzlyJohn gives me something campaign work cannot: a direct view of the full loop from human need to product concept to build to actual adoption. That makes it one of the strongest examples of how I think.
