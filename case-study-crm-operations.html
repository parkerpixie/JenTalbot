case study 05 • CRM adoption + operations

CRM Adoption + Operational Improvement.

A behind-the-scenes case study in helping teams use CRM more effectively through training, troubleshooting, data quality, documentation, and workflow redesign.

See the operating model →Back to case studies

Not every strong case study needs a customer-facing hero image. Sometimes the best proof is a process that became clearer, more usable, and more reliable.

The challenge

A CRM only creates value when people can trust it and know how to use it.

At The Alliance, my work sat across Sales Operations, Member Services, Business Development, CRM support, and data quality. I led company-wide Microsoft Dynamics CRM training, provided individualized troubleshooting, supported reporting and dashboards, maintained critical stakeholder data, documented processes, and helped redesign workflows to reduce manual effort and clarify ownership.

Trainingcompany-wide Microsoft Dynamics CRM training for sales, customer service, and executive leadership teams
Supportindividual troubleshooting for system issues, reporting, dashboards, and data accuracy concerns
Datamaintained and improved member, prospect, and stakeholder data integrity
Processdocumented SOPs, guidelines, workflows, and training materials
Re-engineeringhelped improve workflows, reduce manual effort, clarify ownership, and strengthen coordination
Cross-functionalsupported Business Development, Member Services, sales operations, and leadership needs

Operating model

Technology adoption is a people problem and a systems problem.

01

Listen

Understand where users are confused, where data is unreliable, and which workflows create unnecessary friction.

02

Troubleshoot

Resolve immediate CRM issues while looking for the recurring process or data problem underneath them.

03

Train

Teach the system in language that makes sense to the people using it, not in software-documentation dialect.

04

Document

Create repeatable procedures and guidance so knowledge is not trapped in one person's head.

05

Improve

Use recurring pain points to identify better workflows, cleaner ownership, and opportunities to reduce manual effort.

What this proves

I can improve the operating system behind marketing and sales.

✓

Adoption

Tools become useful when people understand what they are for, trust the data, and can complete their work without fighting the system.

▦

Data discipline

Reporting and automation are only as dependable as the records, definitions, and ownership underneath them.

↻

Process improvement

Recurring support issues often point to workflow design problems worth fixing at the source.

↔

Translation

I am comfortable moving between users, sales teams, marketers, executives, and technical systems without losing the business problem.

The takeaway

This case study shows the other half of marketing automation: the internal systems, data, training, and process design required before sophisticated campaigns can work reliably.
